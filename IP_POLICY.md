# EAIMS Intellectual Property Policy — v1.0

Copyright © 2026 Elias Naserkhaki. All rights reserved except as expressly licensed.

This policy distinguishes six separate matters that MUST NOT be conflated: (1) ownership of original EAIMS intellectual property, (2) public license grants, (3) third-party contributions, (4) external reviews and findings, (5) trademark and branding rights, and (6) governance authority.

## 1. Ownership of original EAIMS intellectual property

EAIMS was independently created and initially authored by **Elias Naserkhaki**. To the extent created by him and not otherwise assigned, copyright and other transferable intellectual-property rights in original EAIMS material remain his property, subject only to rights expressly licensed to the public or to third parties.

Open licensing permits use under the applicable license; it does **not** transfer ownership of the underlying copyright, project identity, official-release authority, domain rights, trademarks, governance authority, or stewardship.

No contribution, review, pull request, issue, sponsorship, implementation, assessment, collaboration, or participation transfers ownership of pre-existing EAIMS intellectual property by implication.

## 2. Public license grants

Unless a file expressly states otherwise, the repository license map in `LICENSE` applies. Rights already granted for a released version under CC BY 4.0 or Apache-2.0 remain effective according to those licenses and are not retroactively revoked.

Subject to applicable law and third-party rights, future EAIMS versions, newly created material, and future derivative works may be released under the same, additional, dual, proprietary, commercial, or different license terms by the person or entity holding the relevant rights.

A public license grant is not a transfer of copyright ownership, authorship, trademark ownership, governance authority, official status, or release-control rights. The copyright notice for EAIMS-owned material does not overwrite ownership of third-party material or CLA-licensed contributions that have not been assigned.

## 3. Mandatory rights instrument for substantive contributions

A **Substantive Contribution** is any proposed third-party text, code, schema, test, translation, data structure, graphic, normative requirement, maturity anchor, assessment content, example, or other copyrightable material that is intended to be incorporated directly into an official EAIMS release and that is more than a trivial correction, factual report, non-copyrightable idea, or de minimis edit.

**No Substantive Contribution from a third party SHALL be incorporated into an official EAIMS release unless, before incorporation, the contributor has completed a project-approved written rights instrument.** The required instrument SHALL be one of the following:

1. a copyright assignment in the form of `COPYRIGHT-ASSIGNMENT.md` or another written assignment approved by Elias Naserkhaki; or
2. a CLA in the form of `CLA.md` or another written agreement approved by Elias Naserkhaki that, at minimum, grants rights sufficiently broad to permit use, modification, reproduction, distribution, sublicensing, relicensing, dual-licensing, commercial licensing, and proprietary relicensing of the contributed material.

Where the project requires a clear sole-ownership chain for official EAIMS copyrightable expression, **assignment is required**. A CLA may leave copyright ownership of the identified Contribution with the contributor while granting EAIMS broad exploitation and relicensing rights; that retained contribution copyright does not create co-ownership of pre-existing or independently authored EAIMS material, joint governance, or EAIMS co-authorship. If assignment is legally unavailable, invalid, incomplete, or impracticable for the relevant rights, the material SHALL NOT be incorporated as third-party authored expression unless Elias Naserkhaki expressly approves an alternative rights basis that provides equivalent practical control to the maximum extent legally possible; otherwise, the project may independently rewrite the underlying idea or finding without copying protected expression.

DCO sign-off, a pull request, an email, an issue comment, public posting, review participation, or verbal permission **does not replace** the required CLA or assignment for a Substantive Contribution.

## 4. Rights required from contributors

A contributor MUST have the legal right to submit the material and MUST NOT submit employer-confidential, client-confidential, proprietary, restricted, unlawfully copied, or otherwise unauthorized material.

The contributor MUST disclose any known third-party rights, employer claims, contractual restrictions, patents believed necessarily implicated, or other encumbrances that could limit EAIMS use or relicensing.

The project may reject any contribution where chain of title, provenance, confidentiality, license compatibility, patent clearance, or relicensing freedom is uncertain. For copyrightable code or other material that may implicate contributor-controlled patent claims, the accepted rights instrument MUST provide the patent permissions required by the applicable project form or another expressly approved written agreement.

## 5. External reviews, probes, findings, and factual input

External reviewers, assessors, feasibility-probe authors, collaborators, and contributors of findings may provide observations, defects, findings, recommendations, facts, requirements, or ideas without becoming authors, co-authors, co-owners, maintainers, governing authorities, or official representatives of EAIMS.

Where EAIMS uses only the underlying fact, idea, finding, or recommendation and independently drafts the resulting EAIMS expression, no ownership of EAIMS text is created merely by providing that input.

Where third-party **copyrightable expression, code, or artifact is directly incorporated** into the repository or an official release, the mandatory rights-instrument rule in Section 3 applies.

A person's role SHALL be recorded only according to the role actually performed, such as `external assessor`, `feasibility-probe author`, `reviewer`, or `contributor of findings`. Such role labels do not create ownership, authorship of EAIMS, governance rights, veto rights, release-approval rights, or representation authority.

## 6. Independent expression and clean-room discipline

Contributions and project text must be independently written. Ideas, facts, methods, public principles, and interoperability facts may inform EAIMS, but protected expression from proprietary or restricted sources MUST NOT be reproduced without permission.

Contributors MUST NOT copy protected wording, tables, graphics, taxonomies, scoring rules, training materials, paid standards text, employer documents, customer documents, or confidential assessment content into EAIMS unless a documented legal right permits incorporation under terms compatible with the intended EAIMS release.

Where ownership or provenance is uncertain, the preferred mitigation is independent clean-room redrafting from uncopyrightable ideas, facts, or functional requirements.

## 7. AI-assisted material

AI assistance does not alter responsibility for originality, provenance, factual review, confidentiality, license compatibility, and rights clearance. Material generated or transformed with AI MUST NOT be accepted merely because its immediate source is an AI system.

## 8. Third-party standards and external sources

Third-party standards, frameworks, regulations, products, and publications may be referenced for factual identification, citation, comparison, mapping, interoperability, or research where legally permissible. Protected text, logos, paywalled tables, or proprietary expression MUST NOT be reproduced without a documented right.

Legally required attribution and bibliographic citation do not make the cited person or organization an EAIMS author, co-owner, governing authority, or endorser.

Statements of alignment, equivalence, certification, accreditation, approval, or endorsement require evidence and explicit project authorization.

## 9. Patents

CC BY 4.0 does not grant patent rights. Apache-2.0 patent rights apply only as stated in that license. Any known patent claim necessarily implicated by a proposed contribution SHOULD be disclosed before acceptance. A rights instrument may include additional patent permissions as expressly stated in that instrument.

## 10. Trademark, branding, and official status

The EAIMS name, logos, source-identifying visual identity, domains, official release designations, and project goodwill are legally and operationally distinct from copyright licensing. See `TRADEMARK.md`.

Open-source or open-content licensing does not grant a right to claim official EAIMS status, endorsement, certification, accreditation, authorship, maintainership, governance authority, or representation authority.

## 11. Governance authority

Copyright ownership, license grants, contribution rights, and governance authority are separate. Governance authority is defined in `GOVERNANCE.md` and is not created by contribution, review, citation, implementation, or public-license use.

## 12. Disputed material

Material with a credible ownership, confidentiality, provenance, infringement, or encumbrance concern may be quarantined, removed, rewritten, or withheld from release while the issue is investigated. Removal does not imply an admission of liability.

## 13. No retroactive claim over third-party rights

EAIMS does not claim ownership of third-party rights merely by policy declaration. Nothing in this policy retroactively converts independently owned third-party material into property of Elias Naserkhaki. Required rights must be obtained in writing before direct incorporation into an official release.

If exclusive or sole ownership is desired but cannot lawfully be obtained, the material MUST either remain external, be used only as factual/idea input, or be independently rewritten without copying protected expression.
