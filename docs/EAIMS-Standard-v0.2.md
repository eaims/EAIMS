# EAIMS Standard v0.1

## 1. Purpose

The Enterprise AI Maturity Standard defines a consistent method for evaluating an organization's ability to select, build, deploy, govern, operate, and improve artificial intelligence systems.

## 2. Scope

EAIMS applies to organizations using or planning to use:

- Predictive machine learning
- Generative AI and large language models
- Retrieval-augmented generation
- AI agents and workflow automation
- AI-enabled products and services
- Third-party AI services
- On-premises, cloud, and hybrid AI platforms

EAIMS evaluates organizational capability. It does not certify the safety, legality, performance, or suitability of a specific model or system.

## 3. Normative language

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, and **MAY** indicate requirement strength.

- **MUST**: mandatory for conformance
- **SHOULD**: recommended unless a justified exception exists
- **MAY**: optional

## 4. Principles

### 4.1 Business alignment
AI initiatives MUST connect to measurable organizational outcomes.

### 4.2 Evidence over perception
Scores MUST be supported by observable evidence.

### 4.3 Responsible AI by design
Security, privacy, safety, fairness, transparency, accountability, and human oversight SHOULD be integrated throughout the lifecycle.

### 4.4 Vendor neutrality
EAIMS MUST NOT require a specific vendor, model, or implementation architecture.

### 4.5 Context sensitivity
Assessments SHOULD account for organization size, sector, regulatory context, risk exposure, and strategic ambition.

### 4.6 Continuous improvement
Maturity is not a one-time score. Organizations SHOULD reassess periodically and after material changes.

## 5. Maturity levels

### Level 0 — AI Unaware
AI capabilities, risks, ownership, and operating practices are absent or unrecognized.

### Level 1 — AI Exploring
Individuals or teams conduct experiments. Outcomes are inconsistent and controls are mostly informal.

### Level 2 — AI Enabled
Selected use cases operate repeatably within departments. Initial architecture, governance, data, and delivery practices exist.

### Level 3 — AI Integrated
Enterprise standards, platforms, governance, lifecycle management, and cross-functional ownership are established.

### Level 4 — AI Optimized
AI is managed as a portfolio. Performance, risk, cost, adoption, and business value are measured and continuously improved.

### Level 5 — AI Native
AI is embedded in the operating model, products, services, decision systems, and organizational learning loops.

## 6. Assessment dimensions

EAIMS assesses nine dimensions:

1. Strategy and Leadership
2. Value and Portfolio Management
3. Data and Knowledge Readiness
4. AI Engineering and Architecture
5. Infrastructure and Platforms
6. MLOps, LLMOps, and Lifecycle Operations
7. Governance, Risk, Security, and Responsible AI
8. Organization, Talent, and Culture
9. Adoption, Process Transformation, and Change

## 7. Assessment requirements

A conforming EAIMS assessment MUST:

- Define the organizational scope
- Identify accountable assessment owners
- Record evidence for each scored capability
- Distinguish current state from target state
- Apply the published scoring method
- Record exceptions and assumptions
- Avoid averaging away critical risk failures
- Produce prioritized improvement actions

## 8. Conformance classes

### 8.1 Self-assessment
Conducted by the organization using EAIMS materials.

### 8.2 Facilitated assessment
Conducted with an internal or external facilitator.

### 8.3 Independent review
Evidence and scoring reviewed by a party independent of the assessed operating unit.

EAIMS v0.1 does not define accredited certification.

## 9. Reassessment

Organizations SHOULD reassess:

- At least annually
- After major organizational restructuring
- After significant AI platform changes
- After material incidents
- Before or after major AI investment programs

## 10. Limitations

A maturity score does not guarantee:

- AI system safety
- Regulatory compliance
- Positive return on investment
- Ethical outcomes
- Security assurance
- Successful organizational adoption

EAIMS is a decision-support framework, not a substitute for professional legal, regulatory, security, or audit advice.
