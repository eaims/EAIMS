# EAIMS Assessor Handbook v0.2

## Purpose

This handbook supports consistent, evidence-based assessments. It does not create an accredited assessor status.

## Before scoring

1. Define organizational boundary, business units, AI-system population, exclusions, period, and intended decisions.
2. Name the sponsor, assessment lead, evidence owners, reviewers, and conflicts of interest.
3. Establish confidentiality, retention, access, and redaction rules. Do not store secrets or personal data in public repositories.
4. Record whether the assessment is self, facilitated, or independently reviewed.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer converging evidence from systems, measured outcomes, operating records, controlled documents, observations, and interviews—in that order. A policy without operational evidence does not prove an operational capability. A tool purchase does not prove adoption or value.

Evidence should be relevant to scope, attributable to an owner, sufficiently current, and resistant to selective interpretation. Use stable evidence IDs; sensitive content may remain in the organization's controlled repository.

## Interview practice

- Ask for recent examples and counterexamples.
- Separate aspiration from current practice.
- Interview business, engineering, data, risk, security, HR, operations, and users where relevant.
- Test whether practices survive personnel changes and apply beyond one successful team.
- Record disagreement rather than forcing consensus prematurely.

## Scoring rule

Select the highest anchor fully supported by evidence. If a Level 4 claim satisfies Level 4 language but lacks the institutional foundations described at lower levels, score the lower defensible level. Assessors must not add points for ambition, spending, number of tools, or executive enthusiasm.

Level 3 or higher requires at least one evidence reference in the executable conformance class. Use `assessment/evidence-catalog.json` as guidance, not as an exhaustive checklist.

## Confidence

- **Low:** primarily perception or unverified statements.
- **Medium:** multiple sources with partial verification.
- **High:** documented, observed, measured, and corroborated.

More than one-third Low-confidence scores makes the result provisional. Confidence never raises a maturity score.

## Not applicable

Exclusion is exceptional. Document why the capability is outside the defined scope, who approved the exclusion, and its effect on the denominator. Never use exclusion to hide a weak capability.

## Challenge and moderation

For facilitated and independent reviews, challenge Level 3–5 claims, critical dimensions, major score disagreements, and evidence older than the stated freshness guidance. Record the original score, proposed score, evidence considered, decision, and decision-maker.

## Reporting

Report calculated and final levels separately, gate adjustments, exclusions, confidence distribution, limitations, material risks, target state, and prioritized actions. Never describe a result as certification, legal compliance, safety assurance, or universal benchmark standing.
