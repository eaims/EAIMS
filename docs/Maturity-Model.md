# EAIMS Maturity Model

## Interpretation

Maturity reflects institutional capability, not the number of tools deployed.

An organization with many disconnected AI pilots may remain at Level 1. An organization reaches higher maturity only when capabilities are repeatable, governed, measured, and improved.

## Level characteristics

| Attribute | L0 | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy | None | Informal interest | Department plans | Enterprise strategy | Adaptive portfolio | AI-native operating model |
| Ownership | None | Individual champions | Local owners | Executive governance | Enterprise accountability | Embedded accountability |
| Delivery | None | Experiments | Repeatable projects | Standard lifecycle | Continuous optimization | Autonomous adaptive delivery |
| Data | Fragmented | Ad hoc access | Priority datasets | Governed enterprise access | Active quality optimization | Intelligent knowledge fabric |
| Risk | Unrecognized | Reactive | Basic controls | Lifecycle governance | Quantified risk management | Continuous assurance |
| Value | Unknown | Anecdotal | Project metrics | Portfolio KPIs | Value optimization | AI-shaped business model |

## Level assignment rule

The overall maturity level SHOULD be the lower of:

1. The weighted aggregate score; and
2. The highest level for which all critical capability gates are satisfied.

This prevents strong performance in low-risk areas from masking critical failures in governance, security, data quality, or lifecycle controls.
