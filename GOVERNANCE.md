## 1. Mission

EAIMS is maintained as an open, vendor-neutral, evidence-grounded, and executable enterprise AI maturity specification. Openness of use, review, and contribution does not transfer ownership of original EAIMS intellectual property, project identity, marks, licensing authority, governance authority, or official-release authority.

## 2. Founding stewardship and reserved authority

**Elias Naserkhaki** is the founder, initial author, and founding steward of EAIMS.

To the maximum extent legally permissible, and unless he expressly delegates a defined power through a written project record, Elias Naserkhaki retains final authority over the official EAIMS project, including:

- **editorial acceptance** or rejection of proposed material;
- **release approval** and designation of official EAIMS versions;
- **licensing decisions** for material for which he holds or controls the relevant rights;
- **dual-licensing, sublicensing, relicensing, commercial licensing, and proprietary relicensing** where permitted by the applicable rights chain;
- determination of the license or licensing model for **future versions and newly created EAIMS material**, subject to previously granted licenses and third-party rights;
- approval of official conformance language, registries, release metadata, project statements, and representation claims;
- appointment, delegation, limitation, suspension, or removal of maintainers and other operational roles;
- final governance decisions, including interpretation of project policy and resolution of governance disputes;
- authorization of uses of EAIMS names and marks where permission is required by `TRADEMARK.md`; and
- protection of project integrity, neutrality, provenance, intellectual-property chain of title, and legal position.

No public process, vote, contribution history, review, sponsorship, issue discussion, pull request, collaboration, implementation, or external participation creates a veto, approval right, governance vote, co-stewardship right, agency, fiduciary duty, partnership, or authority to bind EAIMS unless Elias Naserkhaki expressly grants that authority in writing.

## 3. Previously granted licenses and future licensing

License grants already made for previously released material under CC BY 4.0 or Apache-2.0 are not retroactively revoked and remain governed by their applicable license terms.

For future versions, newly created material, or derivative works for which Elias Naserkhaki holds or controls sufficient rights, he may, to the extent legally permitted and subject to third-party rights, select the applicable license model, including continuation of an existing license, additional licensing, dual-licensing, commercial licensing, or proprietary licensing.

Nothing in this governance policy purports to relicense third-party material beyond the rights actually obtained from the relevant rights holder.

## 4. Roles

### Founding steward

The founding steward holds the reserved authorities in Section 2.

### Maintainers

Maintainers may receive limited operational responsibilities. Delegation is bounded by its written scope, does not transfer ownership or reserved authority, and is revocable unless a binding written instrument expressly states otherwise.

### Contributors

Contributors may submit issues, research observations, findings, proposed wording, code, examples, translations, tests, schemas, or tools. A submission is only a proposal until expressly accepted. Substantive third-party copyrightable material may not enter an official release until the mandatory contribution-rights requirements in `IP_POLICY.md`, `CONTRIBUTOR-RIGHTS.md`, and `CLA.md` / `COPYRIGHT-ASSIGNMENT.md` are satisfied.

### Reviewers, assessors, and external participants

Reviewers, assessors, feasibility-probe authors, collaborators, and other external participants may provide evidence, findings, observations, or recommendations. Their participation does not create EAIMS authorship, co-authorship, ownership, co-ownership, maintainership, governing authority, veto rights, release-approval rights, official-representative status, certification authority, or endorsement.

If their copyrightable text, code, or artifact is directly incorporated into an official release, the mandatory rights-instrument rule applies before incorporation.

## 5. Decision process

1. A proposal, finding, or contribution is submitted or prepared.
2. Provenance, legal/IP status, and contribution-rights requirements are checked where relevant.
3. Technical, methodological, and editorial review occurs as appropriate.
4. The founding steward, or a specifically delegated decision-maker acting within written scope, records a disposition.
5. Material changes accepted into an official release are traceable in release records.
6. Only a release approved under this governance policy may be represented as an official EAIMS release.

Public discussion, consultation, and review are advisory unless a written delegation expressly grants decision authority.

## 6. Conflict of interest and neutrality

Participants in material review SHOULD disclose relevant conflicts through the review process. A disclosure does not itself grant or remove ownership or governance rights.

No organization, sponsor, contributor, reviewer, customer, implementation provider, or other participant may purchase or acquire by participation any right to control the specification, a maturity outcome, favorable scoring, certification status, endorsement, licensing decision, release decision, or governance decision.

## 7. Versioning

- `0.x` — community/research drafts and baselines.
- `1.x` — official 1.x releases and compatible revisions designated through this governance process.
- `2.x` — material structural changes.

Pre-release labels such as `fc`, `alpha`, `beta`, and `rc` are not stable releases unless explicitly designated otherwise.

## 8. Independence of implementation

Third parties may implement or adapt material under the applicable licenses, but independent or modified implementations MUST NOT imply that they are the official EAIMS project, an official EAIMS certification body, or endorsed by EAIMS unless separately authorized in writing.

## 9. No implied transfer or shared control

Nothing in contribution, review, attribution, citation, collaboration, implementation, sponsorship, public licensing, or repository participation transfers copyright, trademark rights, domain rights, project identity, governance authority, or official-release authority by implication.

Any transfer of reserved authority, ownership, or permanent control must be explicit, written, legally effective, and signed by the person holding the relevant right or authority.
